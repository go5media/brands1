Hands-on review · Professional network video

AXIS Q1659Photographic detail.
Professional CCTV.

A large sensor, interchangeable Canon lenses and an impressive amount of detail. We explore what this older specialist camera could bring to a community safety network.

By UK Safety Camera Network17 October 2026Initial daylight assessment
Home / Brands / Axis Communications / Q1659 review

A wide view is useful. Being able to examine a small part of that view afterwards can be even more valuable.

At UK Safety Camera Network, we are interested in cameras that can capture useful evidence across public spaces. The AXIS Q1659 offers an unusual combination: photographic imaging hardware, interchangeable lenses and resolutions up to 20 megapixels. Our early testing revealed impressive detail, alongside practical considerations around power, mounting and outdoor protection.

What we tested: unboxing, initial configuration, manual focus, privacy masking and daylight digital zoom. Night performance, analytics, audio, recording reliability and long-term operation have not yet been fully assessed. Practical observations below come from our own unit and test setup.

01 / Background

An older design with specialist appeal

The Q1659 combines Canon imaging technology with Axis network video technology. It uses a large APS-C image sensor and a Canon EF mount, accepting supported EF and EF-S lenses. Lens choice changes both the viewing angle and the camera’s suitability for a particular scene.[1]

Announced in September 2016, it is now roughly ten years old. Axis discontinued the range in September 2023. Its current support page lists AXIS OS support until 31 December 2029 and hardware support and RMA service until 30 September 2029. Those dates should not be confused with warranty cover for an individual secondhand unit.[2][3]

It remains an interesting proposition where fine detail, lens choice and a carefully designed installation matter more than having the newest camera body.

02 / First impressions

A premium experience from the box

Our first impression was that the Q1659 felt like a premium product before we had even powered it on.

After cutting the security seal, opening the box involved several folding and interlocking flaps. Inside were multilingual instructions, a supplied strap and the camera itself, protected by a cloth bag and two substantial foam supports.

We lifted the complete protected assembly out, removed the supports and slid the bag away. The presentation felt polished and carefully arranged. Packaging does not establish camera performance, but this was a satisfying introduction to the product.

03 / Initial setup

The right power source—and a borrowed lens

Our first PoE connection was not enough

Using our initial standard PoE connection, the camera powered up and allowed us to work through configuration. However, we could not get it fully operating as a camera. Moving it to one of our 30 W PoE+ switches resolved the issue: after signing in with our new credentials, we obtained a video feed.

Fortunately, we already had that switch available for our network’s PTZ cameras. Buyers should check their power equipment before assuming an existing PoE connection will be sufficient. The switch’s 30 W rating describes its supply capability, rather than a constant consumption figure for the camera.

Our photography equipment came in useful

Our particular unit arrived without a lens. Lens-equipped versions were sold, so checking exactly what a listing includes is important.

We had a compatible lens available from the Canon camera we use to photograph installations for our website and marketing. We transferred it to the Q1659 and adjusted focus manually. Once dialled in, it produced a usable image.

The lens was in manual focus mode. This test therefore does not establish autofocus performance with a supported lens operating in autofocus mode.

There was something satisfying about two other parts of our work making this test possible: our photography kit supplied the lens, while our PTZ equipment supplied the power.

04 / Resolution versus movement

Why we chose 11 megapixels

We selected the 11 MP capture mode as a middle ground between resolution and frame rate. We commonly work with 4K cameras, so having an option above that resolution was appealing.

Moving to 20 MP also gives a wider field of view with the same lens. It captures more of the scene, rather than automatically providing a substantial increase in detail on the same distant object. Axis’s published viewing angles reflect this difference.[4]

For us, 11 MP was a sensible starting point. The best permanent setting would depend on the scene, required coverage and movement being recorded. The initial capture-mode selection is also distinct from the resolution selected for an individual video stream.

05 / The standout result

Useful detail inside the wider picture

Initially, we could not find the digital zoom behaviour we wanted. Exploring the interface led us to Viewing Areas. We created an area, opened its properties and enabled PTZ mode. That unlocked digital pan, tilt and zoom for that view.

In the interface we tested, PTZ needed to be enabled for each viewing area where we wanted those controls. The capability was useful once configured, although finding it took some exploration.

In one daylight test, we could read a vehicle’s registration at an estimated distance of more than 50 metres.

We achieved this by digitally cropping and enlarging the image. The camera had not physically moved and the lens had not been optically zoomed. A registration too small to read in the overall view became readable when we examined the captured detail.

That matters to us as a safety network. It demonstrates the potential to retain a useful overview while examining a smaller area without directing a motorised camera away from the wider scene.

Understanding the result: digital zoom reveals information already captured; it does not create new detail. The distance was estimated, and this was a successful daylight test under particular conditions—not a guarantee of plate readability at that distance in every installation. This was not a test of automatic number plate recognition.

Digital PTZ remains limited to the scene visible through the lens. It cannot see outside that field of view or recover detail that was never recorded.

06 / Controls and capabilities

A useful set of tools to explore

We tested privacy masking and found it worked as expected. We also explored controls for exposure, focus options, noise reduction and lighting conditions, while leaving most image settings largely unchanged.

The interface offered stream configuration, Zipstream options and date-and-time overlays. Our unit also had AXIS Fence Guard, Motion Guard, Video Motion Detection and Video Scene Provider installed. Their presence is useful to note, but we have not evaluated their performance.

SD-card edge recording, I/O connections and external audio connections offer further possibilities. We have not yet tested recording reliability or audio operation.

The microphone/line input and audio line output could support a suitable external audio arrangement. For example, the output may be useful for announcements through powered audio equipment; it should not be assumed to drive a passive speaker directly.[4]

07 / The complete installation

Flexibility comes with extra equipment

Outdoor protection needs proper planning

The Q1659 needs a suitable housing for outdoor use. The housing must accommodate the camera body, fitted lens, cables and secure attachment—not simply close around them.

We tried standard box-camera housings during outdoor tests and found the fit extremely tight. The assembly could not be secured properly in the arrangement we tried. Although it felt wedged in place, that is not an adequate basis for permanent installation.

A correctly sized housing is therefore an important part of the purchase, whether supplied by Axis or another suitable manufacturer.

Night operation needs illumination

The removable infrared-cut filter allows infrared-sensitive operation in night mode. The camera has no built-in IR illuminator, so in darkness it needs suitable external illumination, such as an IR illuminator. Where sufficient existing light is available, additional IR may not be necessary.

We have not yet completed night testing, so we cannot offer a hands-on verdict on its low-light performance.

Mounting for awkward locations

The box-camera format gives freedom over lens choice, housing and position. A suitable projecting arm could place the lens beyond a protruding wall, giving it a clear view at an otherwise awkward host location.

That benefit comes from the mounting arrangement. Some domes also support projecting mounts, but a box-camera assembly can be a practical way to achieve the required position.

We are already familiar with separate brackets and adapters on our PTZ installations. Here, the additional consideration is the size and cost of a housing suitable for both the body and lens. The finished system can involve considerably more than purchasing the camera alone.

What impressed us

  • Fine detail in our daylight test.
  • Digital inspection without physically moving the camera.
  • Supported interchangeable Canon lenses.
  • Flexible box-camera installation options.
  • Polished presentation and packaging.

What to plan for

  • Lens inclusion and compatibility.
  • A suitable power source.
  • Securely fitted outdoor housing and mount.
  • External illumination in darkness.
  • Lower frame rates at higher resolutions.
08 / Our early verdict

Promising for the right location

The AXIS Q1659 has given us a strong reason to continue testing. Its most impressive result so far was retaining readable registration detail within a wider daylight scene.

Combined with interchangeable lenses and flexible mounting options, that makes it a promising candidate for selected USCN locations. We would consider it where its detail and flexibility justify the complete installation cost.

Next, we need to assess moving subjects, night performance and recorded footage. For now, our impression is positive: an older specialist camera that can still produce useful detail, provided the installation is designed around its strengths and requirements.
